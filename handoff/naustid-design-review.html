01The idea

Naustið occupies a 1931 timber house on the harbour at Húsavík, clad in vertical bárujárn — corrugated iron. The whole identity is taken from that building rather than chosen: the four colours above were sampled out of the restaurant's own photograph of its façade. Sunlit paint #FAE67A, body #C0A632, the black sign plate #150503, the cool window trim #CFE9EA.

The corrugation survives in the page as the hairline rule — every divider is a repeating vertical tick rather than a solid line. An earlier version rendered every photograph as a canvas rib-raster that resolved into the real image on scroll; that was cut, and the motion below replaced it.

Type is three faces by strict role: Fraunces for display (all caps, -0.035em), Inter for running copy only, Space Mono for every label and figure. There are no box-shadows anywhere on the page and no rounded corners except on call-to-action pills.

03Image drift

No photograph moves with the page. Each sits in a fixed window with an inner wrapper that is deliberately larger than its frame, and that wrapper translates as the frame crosses the viewport. Real <img> tags throughout — no canvas, no WebGL.

Position is normalised to −1…+1 across the crossing, then applied as translate3d(0, −p × drift%, 0). All of it runs on a single shared requestAnimationFrame loop for the whole page, with every measurement read before any transform is written.

Why the overhang is not a round number

The image must always be wider than its window by more than it travels, or its own edge slides into frame at the extremes. Overhang is derived from the drift (drift × 1.6), never typed in by hand, so the two cannot be changed independently. An earlier pass had them disagreeing and the drift-13 slabs bled by 23.8px. Now measured at zero bleed on every frame.

Panel figures drift on the X axis rather than Y, because a panel's vertical position never changes while it crosses the screen — vertical drift there would be frozen.

04Text masks

Display type rises out of an overflow mask on arrival: the inner layer starts at translateY(108%) and rises to zero over 0.95s. Body copy and furniture use a quieter fade-and-lift instead, so the mask reads as emphasis rather than as the page's only gesture.

Icelandic constraint worth knowing

The mask crops at the line box, so ð, þ, g and j clip against its edge unless two things are true: the mask carries .22em of bottom headroom, and the heading's own leading never drops below 0.98. The reference this was taken from sets display leading at 0.83 — copying that put line two's Á and Ú acutes inside line one's descender zone. This is the single most likely thing to break if the type scale is retuned.

07Small screens and reduced motion

What changes below 1024px

  • The track stops being a flex row and becomes a normal block; panels stack.
  • Nothing is pinned, so nothing can trap the reader.
  • Slabs drop from 100svh to 70svh so they do not eat a whole phone screen.
  • Labels step up from 11px to 12px — at phone width 11px read as fine print.

Under prefers-reduced-motion

  • Every frame sits flush with no overhang and no transform.
  • Every mask and lift rests in its visible state — no copy can be stranded.
  • The journey never initialises, on any width.
Fixed during this pass — worth knowing it existed

A pinned panel cannot scroll, so content taller than the viewport is unreachable rather than merely cropped. At 1280×720 the text panels overflowed by 25–62px. Type and row spacing now tighten below 860px of viewport height; measured at zero overflow across 1280×720, 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1536×864.

Weight

708 KB of imagery on a 3× phone, WebP with a 540w rung for the portrait figures and a JPEG fallback. Every photograph belongs to the restaurant — there is no stock imagery on the page, which is also why the layout favours panels and slabs over large free crops.

09What I'd like your read on

These are genuine open calls, not rhetorical. I have a view on most but would rather hear yours first.

01

Is the journey the right length?

6,160px of sideways travel means roughly six full screens of scrolling while pinned, with no way out but forward. Our other build in this lineage runs about three times longer and feels fine, but that is a hotel with more to say. For a restaurant this may already be too much.

02

Text panels at 1180px leave a 130px gutter each side.

So panels read as cards passing a window rather than as full screens. The alternative is full-viewport-width text panels, which is calmer but loses the sense of discrete rooms. I went with the gutter; it is a one-line change either way.

03

Has the concept survived the motion rewrite?

Corrugated iron is the whole idea, and it now appears only as the hairline rules and in the photographs of the building itself. The previous version rasterised every photo into ribs, which was more distinctive and, per the client's read, too weird. Is the hairline enough, or does the corrugation need one more real appearance?

04

Should body copy rise out of the mask too?

Right now only display type gets the mask; body copy fades and lifts. Masking everything is more emphatic and more uniform, but risks the page feeling like one effect applied indiscriminately.

05

Mobile has no equivalent gesture.

Below 1024px the journey simply becomes a stacked document. That is the honest, safe fallback, but it means the phone experience carries none of the signature. Worth designing a separate device for small screens, or is the drift enough there?

06

Drift is 13% on slabs and 9% on figures.

Enough separation to read as a hierarchy, or should the slabs go further?

10Known gaps

  • No preloader. The reference system opens on a percentage counter tied to real image decode. Not built here — the page currently just arrives.
  • Return-to-position verified on 4 of 6 frames. On the other two the test harness could not drive the scroll back to its starting point. The transform is a pure function of the element's position with no accumulated state, and every frame whose scroll did return came back to within 2px, so I read this as a harness limit rather than a bug — but it is not fully proven.
  • Largest Contentful Paint not captured. The observer returned nothing usable, so first contentful paint (1.1s on a throttled phone) is the only load figure I trust.
  • Entering the pin from below has had less testing than entering from above.
  • The page is a concept and is not linked from anywhere; the restaurant has not seen it.
